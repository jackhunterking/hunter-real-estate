# Offering document distribution review

**Prepared:** 2026-07-25
**Scope:** the nine offering documents attached to Lankin Apartment REIT and Epiphany Legacy in the Hunter & Hunter advisory portal
**Purpose:** establish, per document, who it may be distributed to — and get that confirmed in writing by the dealer and by each issuer
**Status:** open. Nothing in this sheet is a determination; every row marked *Confirm* is a question, not a conclusion.

---

## 1. The framework

Every document carries four independent restrictions. It is distributable to a client only if it clears all four. The strictest gate wins.

| Gate | Who sets it | Where the answer lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Issuer's own restriction | Lankin / Epiphany Legacy | The legend printed on the document |
| 2. Securities law treatment | NI 45-106 / NI 31-103 | What the document *is* — marketing material, offering document, or transactional instrument |
| 3. Who may receive an offer | The prospectus exemption relied on | AI exemption vs. OM exemption — the test is applied per person |
| 4. Dealer approval | Parvis Investment Services Inc. (CCO) | Parvis's marketing review policy |

Gate 4 is operative. A dealing representative acts for the dealer. Every client-facing piece — including the website itself — is a sales communication subject to the dealer's review. Nothing in this sheet overrides that.

### On "nothing says I can't"

Neither PDF carries a hard restrictive legend. There is no "For advisor use only," no "Not for distribution to the public," no "Confidential — do not reproduce" on either document. That observation is correct and it is worth stating plainly rather than glossing over.

But absence of a prohibition is not the same as permission, for two reasons:

1. **Gate 1 is the issuer's to answer, not ours to infer.** Issuers routinely maintain a client version and an advisor version of the same piece and rely on the distributing dealer to know which one it received. A missing legend means the question is unanswered, not answered yes.
2. **Gates 2 and 4 apply regardless of what the issuer printed.** Under the OM exemption, marketing material delivered to a prospective purchaser is incorporated by reference into the OM and filed — that obligation attaches to the document's function, not to its legend. And Parvis's approval is required whether or not the issuer restricted anything.

So the honest position on both documents is: **the documents contain advisor-addressed content, carry no restrictive legend, and therefore need an explicit written answer from the issuer and from Parvis.** That is the whole reason this sheet exists.

## 2. Document type reference

| Type | What it is | Default handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Offering Memorandum** | The offering document. Delivery is a regulated event — delivered to a specific identified prospective purchaser at or before signing, with the risk acknowledgement form, and filed with the regulator. Two-business-day cancellation right and rights of action for misrepresentation attach. | Never a self-serve download. Per-person, per-trade, logged delivery tied to KYC/suitability. |
| **Subscription Agreement** | The instrument by which capital is committed. Not marketing. | Only inside a live subscription flow for an identified person who has cleared KYC, suitability, and exemption qualification. |
| **Term Sheet / Offering Summary** | Marketing material. Under the OM exemption, marketing materials delivered to a prospective purchaser must be incorporated by reference into the OM and filed. The issuer's liability extends to it. | Cannot be authored, edited, excerpted, or re-formatted by the dealer. Version control matters; stale copies in circulation are a live problem. |
| **Fact Sheet** | Frequently two documents under one cover — investor-facing pages up front, a dealer/advisor page at the back carrying compensation. | Assess page by page, not document by document. |
| **Investor Presentation / Deck** | Marketing material, same treatment as the term sheet. Issuers typically maintain an advisor version (with compensation) and a client version (without). | Ask which version was supplied. |
| **Report & Financial Statements** | Continuous disclosure to existing unitholders. Not a prospecting tool. | Both funds describe these as available on request. |

## 3. What the documents say about themselves

### 3.1 `legacy-offering-summary-class-a-2026-07-09.pdf`

*Epiphany Legacy Investment Mutual Fund Trust — Offering Summary, Class A Units. Issued July 9, 2026. Five pages. Currently the only document in the portal with an active "Open document" link.*

**Self-classification (p.4).** The page is headed `MARKETING MATERIAL` under `IMPORTANT LEGAL INFORMATION`, and opens: *"This document should be read in conjunction with the following Offering Memorandum."* It also states the document *"is for informational purposes only and does not constitute an offer to sell or a solicitation of an offer to purchase securities. It is not a complete description of the offering,"* directs prospective purchasers to the OM dated July 8, 2026 including Item 10 – Risk Factors, and sets out the two-business-day cancellation right.

That is the issuer placing the document squarely in Gate 2 as marketing material tied to the OM.

**Advisor-addressed content (p.2, "Class A Unit Mechanics").** Three rows matter:

| Row | Value |
|---|---|
| Selling Commission | Up to 8% of subscription proceeds ("Agents' Fees") |
| Trailing Commission | Up to 1% annually of raised proceeds until the Units are redeemed |
| **What Your Client Pays** | A redemption fee applies on early redemption — refer to schedule set out in the Offering Memorandum |

Two distinct issues, worth separating:

- **The compensation rows** disclose what the selling agent earns. In our own portal these facts are already classed `audience: "advisor"` and withheld from the investor-facing key-facts table. The PDF discloses them openly.
- **The row label "What Your Client Pays"** addresses the reader as the advisor. Notably its *content* is benign — it just points at the OM's redemption fee schedule. It is the framing, not the substance, that identifies the intended reader.

**Assessment:** marketing material by the issuer's own label, containing dealer compensation and at least one advisor-addressed row, with no restrictive legend. *Confirm with issuer and Parvis before it remains publicly reachable.*

### 3.2 `lankin-apartment-reit-fact-sheet-q1-2026-class-a.pdf`

*Lankin Apartment REIT — Fund Fact Sheet, Q1 2026, Series A. Three pages.*

**Page 1** reads as investor-facing: fund details, targeted returns (10–14% total, 7–8% distribution), highlights, net calendar year returns, and extensive footnoted caveats on targets, DRIP, and return-of-capital tax treatment.

**Page 2** carries a block headed **`PURCHASE OPTIONS (FOR EMD ADVISORS)`** — the issuer's own words — listing the $5,000 minimum, the redemption fee grid (Year 1: 10%, Year 2: 8%, Year 3: 6%, none after year three), and **8% of Gross Proceeds** compensation.

Also on page 2: *"This is not a solicitation for investments. Investments are sold only to investors that qualify under applicable prospectus exemptions,"* and a paragraph contemplating that *"to the extent this presentation is deemed to constitute an offering memorandum under applicable Canadian securities laws, investors may have statutory rights of action for damages or rescission."*

**Assessment:** a mixed document — client-safe front page, an explicitly advisor-addressed section on page 2. The section header is a labelled section, not a document-level restriction, which is exactly the ambiguity to resolve. *Ask Lankin whether a client version of this fact sheet exists.*

**Incidental:** page 1 lists `Fund Status: Offering Memorandum`, which indicates the OM exemption is in use for this fund. Relevant to question 2 below.

### 3.3 Checklist for any document added in future

1. **Legends** — "For advisor use only," "For dealer use only — not for distribution to the public," "Confidential," "Do not reproduce or distribute."
2. **The word "client"** in a fee table or terms grid → written *for the advisor, about* the client.
3. **Any compensation flowing to the selling agent** — selling commission, trailer, agents' fees, referral fees. The single most reliable marker of advisor-facing content.
4. **"Marketing material"** stamped anywhere → tied to the OM; inherits filing and approval obligations.
5. **Rights of action / cancellation language** → offering-document territory; delivery is a regulated event.
6. **Version and effective date** → if the current version cannot be stated with confidence, no version should be distributed.

## 4. Current state of the platform

Nine documents across two offerings:

| Offering | Document | `visibility` | File attached | What a signed-in user sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy | Offering Summary (term-sheet) | `public` | yes — public bucket | **Open document** |
| Legacy | Offering Memorandum | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Legacy | Class A Unit Fact Sheet | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Legacy | Term Sheet – Class A | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Legacy | Investor Presentation – Class A | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Lankin | Fund Fact Sheet – Class A | `public` | yes — public bucket | **Open document** |
| Lankin | Offering Memorandum | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Lankin | Subscription Agreement | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |
| Lankin | Report & Financial Statements | `approved-investor` | no | "Available through H&H" |

Four properties of the current implementation:

**1. `visibility` is not doing the work it appears to do.** `ProductDetailView.tsx:294` filters only on `visibility !== "private"`, so `public` and `approved-investor` render identically. What determines whether a link appears is *whether a file is attached* — not the tier. Seven of nine documents are protected by accident, not by design.

**2. `approved-investor` is not enforced.** The signed-URL route states this in its own comment (`app/api/hnc-offering-documents/route.ts:19`): entitlement is currently "authenticated portal user," and investor-approval gating "should be layered on before real private offering documents are published." Attaching a file to any of the seven unattached rows would expose it to every signed-in user regardless of accreditation.

**3. The two attached documents are world-readable.** The `offering-public` Supabase Storage bucket is public, and the Legacy summary is additionally served from `/public/capital/docs/`. No login, no accreditation check, no record of who downloaded what. The portal *page* is auth-gated and excluded in `app/robots.ts`, but `robots.ts` disallows page routes only, and the middleware matcher skips any path with a file extension — neither covers the PDF URLs.

**4. Two housekeeping items.** `public/capital/docs/lankin-apartment-reit-fact-sheet-q2-2025.pdf` is a superseded Q2-2025 fact sheet still live at a public URL though nothing references it — a stale marketing document at a reachable address. And `app.offering_compliance_exemptions` is empty: the platform does not record which prospectus exemption each offering relies on, which is the fact that governs who may receive what.

## 5. Questions for Parvis (CCO)

1. The Epiphany Legacy Offering Summary is currently publicly downloadable. It is labelled marketing material by the issuer, discloses selling commission (up to 8%) and trailing commission (up to 1% annually), and includes a row headed "What Your Client Pays." It carries no restrictive legend. **Is it approved for client distribution, or should it be treated as a dealer-use piece?**
2. **Which prospectus exemption(s) am I relying on for each offering — accredited investor, offering memorandum, or both?** Lankin's fact sheet lists "Offering Memorandum" as fund status; Legacy's summary references an OM dated 2026-07-08.
3. If either offering is sold under the OM exemption, **do the offering pages on my own website constitute marketing materials** requiring incorporation by reference into the OM and filing?
4. **What is Parvis's policy on OM delivery?** Must it be delivered by me per-prospect alongside the risk acknowledgement form, or may it be a portal download for an investor whose status has been verified?
5. **Does Parvis require download logging** — who, which version, when — for exempt-market materials, and in what form should those records be retained?
6. Should the portal display any material to a signed-in user **before** their accredited/eligible investor status has been verified, or should the entire document section be gated on verification?

## 6. Questions for each issuer (Lankin, Epiphany Legacy)

Short and answerable in one email:

1. For each piece you have supplied us — offering summary, term sheet, fact sheet, investor presentation — **is it approved for distribution to prospective investors, or is it advisor-use only?**
2. Where a piece is advisor-use only, **do you maintain a client-facing version**, and may we have it?
3. **Which version is current**, and how will you notify us when it is superseded?
4. Do you require that your materials be **hosted behind authentication** rather than at a public URL?
5. For the OM: **what is your required delivery procedure**, and do you need a record of each delivery?

## 7. Where this stands

Seven of nine documents have no file attached, so nothing is currently leaking. The two that are attached are both pieces containing advisor-addressed content — one with a section explicitly headed "FOR EMD ADVISORS," one disclosing selling and trailing commission and addressing "your client" — and both are readable without a login. The `approved-investor` tier intended to protect the remaining seven is not yet implemented.

The next step is not a code change. It is written answers to sections 5 and 6.
