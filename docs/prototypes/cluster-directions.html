Hunter & Hunter — asset network study 04

Three cluster directions

Same discipline as the terminal about which numbers are allowed on screen — different discipline about how many of them appear at once. Each direction below shows one dot per building and lets the shape of the portfolio do the talking, so nothing has to be read to be understood.

The relationship terminal is still live and untouched. Nothing has been removed. It stays under Investments → Asset network and is marked as in progress while these three directions are evaluated. Whichever one you pick becomes the investor-facing view; the terminal can stay behind it as the analyst view.

Everything below is drawn from these figures — nothing else

02 · Ownership orbit “I own two funds, 60/40, and 39 buildings sit under them.” Best — a circle is the one shape that never needs a wide screen Low — geometry is fixed, no simulation Radial charts invite people to read angles as amounts; capital arc is the only angle that means money
03 · Lens “Same 39 buildings, sorted six different ways.” Best — groups become stacked rows Low — one layout function, one tween Least spectacular of the three; earns trust instead of attention

If you want one recommendation

Ship 03 as the default and 01 as the hero. The lens is the one that survives a sceptical investor — the same 39 dots re-sort in front of them, so the count is self-evidently not invented, and it degrades to stacked rows on a phone without losing anything. The cloud is what makes somebody stop scrolling. They share one data model and one dot renderer, so building both is roughly the cost of one and a third. Direction 02 is the one to hold back: it is the most beautiful and the easiest to misread, because every radial chart trains people to read angle as value and only one of its angles means anything.
