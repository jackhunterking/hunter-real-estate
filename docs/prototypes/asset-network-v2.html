The constellation and the terminal, rebuilt on data we actually hold

You picked the constellation and the clustered-dot view. Both are here, each with three variations you can switch between. The difference from v1 is underneath: nothing in these views claims to know money that has reached you, because we have no way to know it.

Admin only · not shipped

The thing v1 got wrong

Gold particles streaming toward a “$2,383 a month flowing in” figure implied we were tracking payments. We are not, and there is no field in the portal that would let us. What we hold is what you committed and what each fund has published — so the animation now carries the fund’s published distribution schedule, and the headline numbers are commitments and unit counts, never receipts. Everything below is built from the ledger in the next block.

What the portal actually has

  • Your committed amount and unit countamount, shareQuantity, shareClassId per position
  • Position status and its datesdraft → submitted → compliance review → accepted → funded, with submittedAt / updatedAt
  • Published targets, verbatim and sourcedtarget return, target distribution, unit price, minimum — each with a source and an as-of date
  • Trailing return seriesLankin by quarter, Epiphany by year — different periods, so never one axis
  • Fund scale and progressAUM, offering size, amount raised, funding percent, total units
  • Distribution and valuation frequencyLankin monthly, Epiphany quarterly — a schedule, not a payment record
  • Every buildingaddress, city, province, coordinates, asset class, condition, verification status; unit count on 12 of 39
  • Data freshnessdataAsOf, dataPeriodLabel, updateCadence, verifiedAt — enough to know when a figure has gone stale

What it does not have — and must never imply

  • Cash actually receivedno distribution payment records, no dates, no amounts
  • What your position is worth todayno NAV per unit over time, no mark-to-market
  • Realised gain or lossnothing to compute it from
  • Per-building incomeno rent roll, no NOI, no occupancy by asset
  • Confirmation a distribution was paidonly the frequency the fund publishes
  • Tax recordsno slips, no allocations, no return of capital split

The Constellation

Three variations · switch with the mode buttons

You at the centre, funds around you, their buildings beyond. The three modes keep the same skeleton and change what the picture is about: what you own, what you committed, or how well-evidenced it is. The gold pulse is the published distribution schedule — Lankin monthly, Epiphany quarterly — running on a compressed clock. It marks a date the fund says it pays on. It is not a payment.

Constellation

Structure

What you hold and what sits inside it. Buildings carry their real asset class as a shape — disc for residential, square for commercial — so composition reads without a second colour scale.

Capital

Sized by your committed dollars, with each fund’s raise shown as an arc: Epiphany is 55.9% of its offering size away, a real published figure. The available offering stays grey until you add it.

Provenance

The trust view. Buildings coloured by verification status, funds ringed by data freshness. It is the one that can tell you something uncomfortable — and it does.

Keeping it honest

No figure here is money received. Commitments and units are yours; everything else is the fund’s own published number with its as-of date attached. Hover anything to see the date.

The Terminal

Three variations · dots and clusters

The dots-and-clusters view you liked, given a proper instrument rail: both funds with their real published figures, their own trailing-return sparkline, and a freshness stamp. The plot beside it clusters the same 39 buildings three different ways — by geography, by what they are, and by what condition they are in. Dense, dated, and sourced, which is what makes a terminal read as trustworthy rather than showy.

Terminal

Geography

True coordinates, clustered where addresses share a block. Ten Epiphany buildings sit on one Lethbridge block, and the count badge is the honest way to show that rather than one dot pretending to be one asset.

Exposure

Province against asset class. It answers the question geography hides: you are 29 residential and 10 commercial, and almost all of the commercial is in one Alberta city.

Condition

Stabilised, commercial, new construction, value-add — the published condition of every building. This is the closest thing to a risk picture the data legitimately supports.

Keeping it honest

The two sparklines never share an axis: Lankin publishes quarterly and Epiphany annually, and drawing them on one scale would invent a comparison neither fund made.

The rule that keeps this shippable

Every label in both views had to survive one test: could we defend it to someone asking where the number came from? That rules out a whole vocabulary, and the substitutes are not weaker — they are just true.

Never“$2,383 a month flowing in”
Instead“$370,000 committed · 48,825 units”
Never“Income received” / “Earnings”
Instead“Target distribution, published Q2 2026”
NeverA payment landing in an animation
InsteadA scheduled distribution date, labelled as scheduled
Never“Portfolio value” / “Up 8.2%”
Instead“Fund’s published 2025 return: 8.20%”
NeverA figure with no date on it
InsteadEvery figure carries its as-of date and goes stale visibly

First

One route, admin-gated, flag-controlled

/hunter-advisory/network behind the same canAccessPath gate as the admin console, plus NETWORK_VIEW_ENABLED in lib/capital/feature-flags.ts. Both views live on it as tabs — they share a data layer, so building both costs barely more than one.

Then

A single read, straight from the portfolio’s own sources

Positions from dataset.investments, offerings from getPublishedOfferings, coordinates from buildMapProperties, freshness from dataAsOf and updateCadence. If the portfolio page cannot show a number, neither view gets to invent it.

Then

Make staleness visible rather than quiet

Provenance mode already flags that Epiphany’s figures are past their quarterly review date. That belongs in the admin view permanently — it is the single most useful thing either visualisation does for you as the operator.

Open

Whether the schedule pulse survives review at all

It is honest and it is labelled, but it is still an animation about money on a page an investor could one day see. If Parvis would rather it did not exist, the constellation works without it — the web itself carries the idea. Worth asking before it becomes load-bearing.

Buildings, coordinates, asset classes, conditions, verification statuses, published targets, trailing returns, AUM, offering sizes and as-of dates are read from the Lankin and Epiphany records in supabase/seed/offerings/. Only the two commitment amounts are invented, to give the views something to be about. Nothing shown is money received, current value, or a guarantee.
